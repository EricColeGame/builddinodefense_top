## Core Pillars of Creature-Based Perimeter Security

Relying exclusively on automated metal turrets drains massive amounts of ammunition and electrical power. Early to mid-game players often lack the industrial infrastructure required for heavy artillery, making biological defenses essential. A successful biological defense framework divides tames into specialized operational roles rather than clustering random predators around a doorway.

```
Outer Tripwire Layer (Detection / Slowing)
           │
           ▼
Roof & Tower Snipers (Ranged Debuff / Spit Turrets)
           │
           ▼
Inner Yard Enforcers (High-DPS Swarm / Pack Killers)
           │
           ▼
Vault Guardians (Heavy Tank / Crowd Control)
```

To establish a resilient perimeter, your defense system must incorporate three core functions:

*   **Early Warning and Crowd Slowing:** Small, fast creatures that alert your tribe or inflict debilitating status effects to break an enemy sprint.
*   **Stationary Ranged Suppression:** Tames positioned above ground level acting as biological turrets to bombard invaders from safety.
*   **Heavy Response Units:** High-health brawlers configured to neutralize bulkier threats like Carnos, Allosaurs, or aggressive intruders before structures take damage.

## Early-Game Defense: Deploying the Dilophosaur Swarm

New survivors often underestimate common coastal creatures, yet the humble Dilophosaur remains one of the most cost-effective defensive assets in the game. When you establish your initial settlement, grouping 4 to 10 Dilophosaurs together creates a lethal defensive net. 

Their primary strength lies in their venomous spit attack. The projectile blinds targets and inflicts a significant movement penalty, rendering fast predators like Raptors sluggish and disoriented.

Community reports frequently highlight how easily a coordinated group of 6 to 8 low-cost spitters can bring down a stray Carnotaurus by chaining venom debuffs. Because Dilophosaurs tame rapidly with basic raw meat and slingshots, replacing losses takes minimal effort.

To optimize your early-game sentry squad, focus on wild levels and basic enclosure breeding. Knocking out Dilophosaurs requires only a few stones launched from a slingshot or a wooden club. Once secured, feed them raw meat or prime meat to maximize their starting stats.

### The "Living Spit Turret" Roof Design

One classic tactical setup involves positioning Dilophosaurs along your base rooftop or atop isolated pillar platforms. By overloading their inventory with heavy stone, you encumber them to 100% capacity. 

Once fully encumbered, set their behavioral stance to **Aggressive** and target range to **High**. Because their weight prevents them from moving, they cannot leap off your roof or chase targets across the map. Instead, they act as permanent spit turrets, continuously launching blinding venom at any hostile creature that wanders near your foundation walls.

## Whistle Settings, Leashing, and Behavioral Mechanics

Improper behavioral settings are the leading cause of base defense failures. If left on default settings, an aggressive guard dino will chase a runaway Parasaur or flying Pteranodon miles into the wilderness, leaving your compound completely undefended.

To build a dino defense protect base design that remains structurally sound over time, master behavioral whistles and anchor tames using leash structures or hitching mechanics.

### Essential Setting Combinations

*   **Neutral (Default Guard):** The creature only retaliates when it or a nearby tribe member/tame takes damage. Best for fragile yard tames and breeding stock.
*   **Aggressive + Over-Encumbered:** Ideal for stationary ranged guards on platforms. They fire at everything within range without moving out of position.
*   **Aggressive + Dino Leash:** Keeps roaming packs inside an invisible circular boundary. They aggressively shred anything stepping inside the radius without wandering off.
*   **Follow Anchor Technique:** Command your mobile guard pack (e.g., 5 Raptors) to follow a high-health, passive herbivore (such as an anchored Carbonemys or Trike) parked in your courtyard. Set the Raptors to **Aggressive** or **Neutral**. When they finish fighting off an attacker, their follow AI forces them to return directly to the anchored turtle.

| Setting Name | Aggression Setting | Follow Setting | Primary Risk | Mitigation Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rooftop Sniper** | Aggressive | None | Falling off edges | Over-encumber with stone or place railings |
| **Courtyard Pack** | Neutral or Attack My Target | Follow Passive Anchor Dino | Chasing lures out of base | Set follow distance to "Lowest" on tank tame |
| **Choke Point Brawler** | Neutral | None | Kited by ranged snipers | Enclose within double-door airlock gates |
| **Moat Sentinel** | Aggressive | Follow Anchored Raft/Pillar | Drowning or swimming away | Use dino leash submerged to limit radius |

## Fortifying Structural Bases with Dinosaur Traps

Biological guards perform best when integrated directly into your structural engineering. Rather than letting guards wander open fields, construct choke points, funnels, and defensive moats that force attackers into the line of fire.

When designing your perimeter, integrate natural dinosaur traps directly into your compound walls:

1.  **Double-Layer Wooden or Stone Spikes:** Place an outer ring of wooden spike walls 10 foundations out from your main structure. Spikes damage attacking wild carnivores while your elevated spitters rain projectiles from above.
2.  **Airlock Gateways:** Build your entryways with double Dinosaur Gateways. If an aggressive carnivore attempts to charge your gate during entry, yard guards stationed inside the airlock can eliminate it without opening access to your main storage vaults.
3.  **Raised Catwalk Firing Lines:** Construct elevated wood or stone catwalks along the interior rim of your perimeter wall. Station stationary spitters along these catwalks so they shoot over your spikes while remaining safe from ground-based melee attacks.

## Sustenance, Breeding, and Long-Term Base Maintenance

A defensive garrison requires maintenance to prevent starvation and decay. If you leave dozens of carnivores on aggressive wandering without adequate food supplies, their health will drop, leaving them vulnerable to the next raid or wild spawn.

To ensure your defenses remain active while you are offline:

*   **Install Centralized Feeding Troughs:** Ensure every guard creature stands within the radius of a stone or Tek feeding trough. Carnivores consume raw meat, cooked meat, or fish meat automatically when hungry.
*   **Automate Meat Gathering:** Set a breeding pair of high-weight carnivores on aggressive wander inside a closed, walled courtyard populated with passive dodos or phippers, or run regular meat runs with a high-damage mount to top off troughs weekly.
*   **Establish a Breeding Reserve:** Keep your primary breeding pairs (the biological templates for your guard pack) secured inside an insulated, locked interior room on **Passive**. If an Alpha Rex or raiding party wipes out your courtyard defenders, your breeding stock remains untouched, allowing you to hatch a replacement pack in hours.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can Dilophosaurs defend against large carnivores like an Alpha Raptor or Rex?
Individually, no. A single Dilophosaur has low baseline health and will die quickly to high-tier carnivores. However, a pack of 8 to 12 high-level Dilophosaurs utilizing elevation and venom slowing effects can defeat mid-tier threats like Carnos. For Alpha Raptors or wild Rexes, you need to back up your spitters with heavier front-line anchors like a high-level Baryonyx, Carnotaurus, or Therizinosaurus behind protective spike walls.

### How do I stop my base defense dinos from running away when attacking?
The most reliable method is the **Follow Anchor** technique. Set your defensive pack to follow a durable, high-health dinosaur (such as a Carbonemys or Trike) that is set to **Passive** and weighed down with stone. Even if your attack pack rushes out to kill an attacker, their follow behavior pulls them right back to the anchored dinosaur once the threat dies. Alternatively, deploy a **Dino Leash** to restrict their roaming boundary.

### What is the best stance to build a dino defense protect base perimeter?
The best stance depends on creature positioning. For elevated ranged snipers on roofs, use **Aggressive** while overloading their weight so they cannot move. For ground-level courtyard packs, use **Neutral** linked to an anchored tame, or place them on **Aggressive** exclusively within an enclosed yard bound by an active Dino Leash. Never leave roaming guards on unmonitored Aggressive without physical barriers, or wild creatures will easily lure them away.
